This Week 3 project applies formal statistical hypothesis testing to the Titanic passenger dataset to determine whether survival was independent of passenger sex. The research question was selected because the earlier exploratory analysis showed a large difference in survival between female and male passengers. The null hypothesis states that sex and survival are independent, while the alternative hypothesis states that the two variables are associated. A significance level of 5% was selected before testing. The primary method is Pearson’s chi-square test of independence, which is appropriate because both variables are categorical and the expected cell counts are sufficiently large. Fisher’s exact test is included as a robustness check, and a two-proportion z-test directly compares the female and male survival proportions. The analysis also reports 95% confidence intervals and the phi coefficient so that the result is interpreted using both statistical significance and effect size.

The 891-passenger dataset contains 233 female survivors out of 314 females and 109 male survivors out of 577 males. Female survival was approximately 74.2%, compared with approximately 18.9% for males. The statistical tests produce extremely small p-values, providing strong evidence against the null hypothesis of independence. The observed difference in survival proportions is approximately 55 percentage points, and its confidence interval remains clearly above zero. The phi coefficient also indicates a strong association. Five supporting visualizations communicate the result from different perspectives: survival rates with confidence intervals, outcome composition, observed versus expected counts, the confidence interval for the difference, and the association effect size.

The conclusion is that passenger sex and survival were strongly associated in this dataset. However, the result must not be interpreted as proof that sex alone caused survival differences. Passenger class, age, family structure, and other factors may confound the relationship. A future analysis could use logistic regression or stratified tests to examine these variables simultaneously.
