This Week 2 project extends a basic data-cleaning workflow into advanced data visualization and storytelling using the public Titanic passenger dataset. The purpose is not simply to create attractive charts, but to build a coherent visual narrative that can be understood by a non-technical audience. The analysis begins with a grouped comparison of survival rates by sex and passenger class, establishing the most visible differences in outcomes. It then examines age distributions with a violin plot and sampled observations, showing how survivors and non-survivors differ while also highlighting substantial overlap. The third visualization uses an annotated heatmap to combine age bands and passenger class, demonstrating why interactions between variables can be more informative than isolated comparisons. The fourth chart explores age and fare together using a scatter plot with a logarithmic fare axis. Passenger class and survival are incorporated visually to reveal socioeconomic separation and the long-tailed nature of ticket prices. Finally, an empirical cumulative distribution function compares fare distributions for survivors and non-survivors, providing a bin-free view of how the two groups differ across the entire fare range.

The visualization choices are intentional: bar charts support direct comparison, violin plots communicate distributions, heatmaps reveal interactions, scatter plots expose relationships between multiple variables, and ECDFs compare complete distributions. Takeaway-oriented titles, direct labels, descriptive captions, and restrained legends are used to reduce cognitive load. The analysis finds that survival was strongly associated with sex and passenger class, with age and fare adding important context. The report also emphasizes that these are observational associations rather than proof of causation. The repository is organized into source code, figures, data and documentation so the workflow can be reproduced and extended. Overall, the project demonstrates how thoughtful chart selection and narrative sequencing can turn a complex dataset into a clear analytical story suitable for business or scientific communication.
